## Home Neighborhood

**Colonia Doctores** — a lively, slightly gritty, deeply proud central Mexico City neighborhood, famous as the home of Arena México, the world's most iconic lucha libre venue. This is the in-world reason lucha libre culture (and masks) are simply part of everyday life for Ajolote — and the canon reason his superhero identity takes the form it does. Ajolote, Xolo, and Abuela live in a vecindad a few streets from the arena.

## Recurring (Everyday) Locations

- **Neighborhood streets** — the daily map of Ajolote's world: colorful facades, overhead cables, street vendors, stray dogs, and neighbors who know everyone's business.
- **The vecindad** — the shared-courtyard building where Ajolote, Xolo, and Abuela live alongside several other families, including Doña Carmen. A central patio makes it easy for neighbors to see (and comment on) each other's lives.
- **The mercado** — the covered indoor market a few blocks away, home to Don Pepe's fruit-and-juice puesto and dozens of other fixed stalls.
- **The tianguis** — the temporary open-air street market that takes over a nearby avenue on set days of the week; different vendors and rhythm from the fixed mercado.
- **The taquería on the corner** — a small, always-open spot that doubles as a neutral meeting place for characters across the neighborhood.
- **Metro Balderas** — the nearest Metro station, a frequent source of mysteries (lost items, strange sounds, overheard conversations) and a fast way to reach the rest of the city.
- **The neighborhood park** — a small plaza-park with benches, a kiosco, and just enough shade trees to host a stakeout.

## Special-Episode Locations

- **The Zócalo** — the historic central plaza; setting for citywide celebrations like Día de Muertos.
- **Chapultepec** — the great forest/park, castle, and zoo; a good setting for bigger, more adventurous episodes.
- **Coyoacán** — colorful colonial-era neighborhood known for La Casa Azul and its lively market and plazas; occasional weekend-trip setting.
- **Xochimilco** — the canals, chinampas (floating gardens), and trajineras; natural home for water- and ecology-themed episodes.
- **Museums** (e.g. Museo Nacional de Antropología, Templo Mayor) — occasional field-trip episodes tying directly into Mexica/Aztec history topics.

## Topics

- Día de Muertos — traditions, ofrendas, family remembrance.
- Lucha libre — tied directly to Ajolote's identity and Colonia Doctores.
- The Metro — how it works, its role in daily life.
- Chinampas — Xochimilco's floating gardens and ancient farming technique.
- The Aztecs/Mexica — history embedded in the city's geography and museums.
- Local animals — street dogs, market animals, native species like the axolotl.
- Mexican food — markets, home cooking, regional dishes.
- Volcanic geography — the valley of Mexico's volcanic origins.
- Water in CDMX — supply, scarcity, historic canal systems.
- Markets — mercados vs. tianguis, and the people who run them.
- Mexican myths — legends and folklore woven into mysteries.
- Local architecture — vecindades, colonial buildings, street art.
- Ajolote's superhero delusion — the permanent, unresolved question of his powers.
- Xolo's origin — how a talking dog ended up as Ajolote's best friend (kept vague/mysterious).
- Abuela's cooking — recipes, traditions, and the comfort of home.
